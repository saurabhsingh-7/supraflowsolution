Zoho Books vs Tally: Which Is Right for Your Business? (2026)

Updated 24 September 2026 · By the Supraflow Solutions consulting team · 11 min read

Short answer: Both are fully GST-compliant, so compliance is not the deciding factor. Tally Prime is desktop software bought once (₹22,500 Silver, ₹67,500 Gold, ex-GST) — best for inventory-heavy businesses, unreliable internet, and accountant-led teams. Zoho Books is a cloud subscription priced per organisation (₹749–₹7,999/month, ex-GST) — best for remote teams, service businesses, and anyone already using other Zoho apps.

On cost, the honest answer surprises people: over five years Tally is cheaper at 1 user, Zoho Books is cheaper at 3–5 users, and Tally becomes cheaper again at 10+ users. It is a U-curve, not a straight line — because Zoho Books charges per organisation while Tally Gold gives unlimited LAN users.

Most comparisons of these two declare a single winner. That is the wrong shape of answer. They are built on opposite architectures, and which one is cheaper genuinely reverses depending on how many people need access.

This page shows the working, with prices verified from both vendors in September 2026.

1. The one difference that decides most cases

Before features, before price: Tally is desktop software. Zoho Books is a web application.

Tally installs on a machine in your office. Your data sits on your hardware. Internet is needed only for specific things — e-invoicing, GST portal sync, remote access.

Zoho Books runs in a browser. Your data sits in Zoho's cloud. You reach it from any device, anywhere, with no VPN or hosting setup.

This single fact settles the decision for a lot of businesses before any other factor. If your office internet is unreliable, cloud accounting will frustrate you daily and no feature list changes that. If your team works across locations or from home, desktop-only accounting will frustrate you just as much.

Cost is a U-curve, not a straight line. Tally wins at one user, loses badly in the three-to-five band, then wins decisively again past ten users.

The reason is structural: Zoho Books charges per organisation, so going from one user to three costs nothing extra on Standard. But Tally charges per concurrent access model — the moment you need a second simultaneous user you jump from ₹22,500 Silver to ₹67,500 Gold, tripling the licence. Past ten users, Gold's unlimited LAN seats become the bargain instead.

If you are anywhere near these boundaries, run the numbers for your user count before deciding. A generic "Tally is cheaper long-term" claim is only true at the two ends of the curve.

4. GST filing: both compliant, differently convenient

Both are fully GST-compliant. Both generate GSTR-1 and GSTR-3B, handle e-invoicing with IRN, and produce e-way bills. Anyone telling you one is "more compliant" is selling something.

The difference is mechanical:

  • Zoho Books is a registered GST Suvidha Provider. It pushes data straight to GSTN, so you file without leaving the software.
  • Tally uses Connected GST. Recent releases upload and sign GSTR-1 and GSTR-3B with EVC or DSC from inside Tally, and auto-reconcile GSTR-2A/2B. For years this was an export-then-upload workflow; that gap has narrowed sharply.

Honest verdict: Zoho has a small edge on hands-off automation — return-ready data assembled continuously rather than at filing time. Tally gives more control over the data before it leaves your hands, which high-volume businesses and their CAs often prefer. If you file monthly and hate switching to the portal, Zoho's convenience compounds. If your bookkeeper has filed from Tally for a decade, it does not.

5. Inventory: Tally's clearest advantage

This is where the comparison stops being close.

Tally handles multi-godown stock, batch and expiry tracking, bills of materials, job work in and out, and ageing analysis natively. It was built for Indian trading and manufacturing businesses and it shows.

Zoho Books handles items, warehouses and reorder alerts competently — enough for services and light trading. For real depth you add Zoho Inventory, which is a separate subscription, and for manufacturing you are looking at a custom Zoho Creator build or a proper ERP.

If you run multiple godowns with daily stock movement, batch-tracked pharma, or any manufacturing with a BOM, Tally's inventory depth is a genuine reason to choose it — and no amount of cloud convenience offsets it.

6. Where Zoho Books pulls ahead

  • Multi-location and remote teams. Everyone works on the same live data from anywhere, with no VPN, no Tally.NET, no hosting bill.
  • CRM integration. Native Zoho CRM link means a closed deal creates the invoice automatically, and your sales rep sees outstanding payments before a meeting. With Tally this needs a connector and ongoing maintenance.
  • Automation. Recurring invoices, payment reminders, bank rules and approval workflows are built in, no scripting required.
  • Client experience. Payment links on invoices, a self-service customer portal, and native mobile apps.
  • Multi-currency. Available from Professional — important if you invoice foreign clients.

The default for most growing Indian SMEs: if you are service-led with a small team, start on Zoho Books — the free plan is genuinely usable below ₹25 lakh revenue. If you are inventory-led, start on Tally. They integrate well enough that this is not a permanent marriage either way.

8. Can you use both?

Yes, and plenty of businesses do — usually Zoho CRM for sales with Tally for accounts, or Zoho Books for invoicing with Tally for statutory filing because the CA insists.

It works, but understand the cost: you are paying for two systems and someone is reconciling between them. That reconciliation is exactly where errors creep in. We generally recommend picking one as the source of truth and syncing into the other, rather than maintaining both by hand.

9. Migrating from Tally to Zoho Books

If you decide to move, the migration is well-trodden. What actually matters:

  1. Chart of accounts mapping. Tally's ledger groups do not map one-to-one onto Zoho Books account types. This mapping sheet is the single most important artefact of the migration — get it wrong and every report afterwards is wrong.
  2. Opening balances, not full history. Most businesses migrate opening balances at a financial year boundary and keep Tally read-only for historical lookups. Migrating years of transactions is possible but rarely worth the cost.
  3. Master data first. Customers, vendors, items and tax rates go across before any transaction does, with de-duplication on the way.
  4. Reconcile before go-live. Trial balance in Tally must match trial balance in Zoho Books to the rupee. If it does not, you are not ready to switch.
  5. Pick a clean cut-over date. The start of a quarter or financial year, never mid-month.

A straightforward migration for a small business takes one to two weeks. Complex multi-GSTIN setups with inventory take longer.

Frequently asked questions

Is Zoho Books or Tally better for GST?

Both are fully GST-compliant and neither is more compliant than the other. Zoho Books has an edge on filing convenience because it is a registered GST Suvidha Provider and files GSTR-1 and GSTR-3B directly to GSTN. Tally's Connected GST also files from within the software using EVC or DSC, and gives more control over the data before submission. The practical gap between them has narrowed considerably.

Which is cheaper, Tally or Zoho Books?

It depends on team size, and the answer reverses twice. Over five years excluding GST: at 1 user Tally Silver costs ₹40,500 versus ₹44,940 for Zoho Books Standard, so Tally wins narrowly. At 3 to 5 users Zoho Books is cheaper by ₹31,000 to ₹76,000, because it is priced per organisation while Tally requires the ₹67,500 Gold licence for multi-user access. At 10 or more users Tally Gold becomes cheaper again by ₹58,000 or more, because it allows unlimited LAN users.

Can Zoho Books replace Tally completely?

For service businesses, agencies, consultancies and light trading, yes — Zoho Books covers invoicing, expenses, banking, GST filing and financial reporting fully. For inventory-heavy businesses it depends: Zoho Books plus Zoho Inventory covers most trading and distribution needs, but for manufacturing with multi-level bills of materials, job work or batch-and-expiry tracking, Tally's native depth is hard to replace without a custom build.

Does Zoho Books work offline?

No. Zoho Books is a cloud application and needs an internet connection; the mobile apps support limited offline viewing only. Tally runs fully offline and only needs connectivity for e-invoicing, GST filing, Connected Banking and remote access. If your internet is unreliable, this alone may decide the question.

Is Zoho Books free?

Yes, for businesses with annual revenue under ₹25 lakh. The free plan includes 1 user plus 1 accountant, up to 1,000 invoices per year, GST filing, bank reconciliation and over 50 reports. It is a genuine free tier with no time limit, not a trial. Paid plans start at ₹749 per organisation per month on annual billing, excluding GST.

Will my CA be able to work with Zoho Books?

Almost certainly. Zoho Books includes a free accountant login on every plan, and most Indian CAs now work across both tools. That said, Tally remains more universally familiar among chartered accountants, so if your CA relationship is central to your compliance, confirm their comfort level before switching rather than after.

How long does migrating from Tally to Zoho Books take?

A straightforward migration for a small business takes one to two weeks: chart of accounts mapping, master data transfer for customers, vendors and items, opening balances at a financial year boundary, and a trial balance reconciliation before go-live. Multi-GSTIN setups with inventory take three to six weeks. Most businesses migrate opening balances rather than full transaction history and keep Tally read-only for historical lookups.

Do I need to pay Tally's TSS every year?

Not strictly — TallyPrime keeps working without it. But without an active TSS subscription you stop receiving product updates and lose connected services including e-invoicing, e-way bill generation, Connected Banking and remote access. Since GST rules change regularly, most businesses treat TSS as a required recurring cost rather than optional. It is ₹4,500 per year for Silver and ₹13,500 for Gold, excluding GST.
