Odoo ERP Implementation Cost in India (2026)

Updated 9 November 2026 by the Supraflow Solutions consulting team, 8 min read

Short answer: Odoo Community is free to licence but still needs hosting and implementation. Odoo Enterprise costs ₹580 to ₹1,350 per user per month in India on annual billing, before 18% GST, depending on plan tier. Implementation by a partner is a separate one-time fee, typically ₹1,00,000 to ₹2,50,000 for a single-module rollout, and ₹6,00,000 to ₹15,00,000+ for a manufacturing deployment with custom development and multi-warehouse logistics.

A realistic first-year budget for a 10-user team on multi-module Enterprise (Sales, Inventory, Accounting) is ₹1,20,000 to ₹1,60,000 in licences + ₹2,50,000 to ₹4,00,000 in implementation - roughly ₹3.7 to ₹5.6 lakh before GST.

Odoo pricing pages advertise the licence clearly and the implementation almost never. Licence cost is also where Odoo differs most from Zoho: there is a genuinely free self-hosted edition, which changes the maths in ways a straight per-user comparison misses.

This page breaks down both halves, with the working shown.

1. Odoo licence cost in India

Odoo prices India in its India / South Asia regional tier, billed in INR. These are per user per month, on annual billing, excluding 18% GST. Checked against Odoo's India pricing page on 9 November 2026. Odoo runs first-year promotional rates that rise in year two, so always confirm the current figure before quoting a client.

Odoo's first-year prices are promotional and increase from year two (typically by 20 to 30%). Monthly billing costs more than annual and is only available on cloud hosting. GST at 18% is added on top, recoverable as input tax credit if your business is GST-registered.

The edition mistake that costs the most

Community looks free, but "free" only covers the licence. Hosting, security patching, upgrades and implementation still cost the same regardless of edition, and Community lacks Studio, full accounting localisation in some regions, and official support. For most Indian SMBs the real saving from Community is marginal once you account for the dev time spent maintaining a self-hosted instance - it tends to make sense only when you already have an in-house Odoo developer on payroll.

Conversely, jumping straight to Custom for Studio access you will not use for a year is the mirror-image mistake. Start on Standard, upgrade to Custom when a specific customisation need (not a hypothetical future one) shows up.

2. What implementation actually costs

Partner fees are a one-time project cost, separate from licences. Odoo's range is wider than Zoho's because the typical Odoo project touches more interdependent modules - Inventory valuation feeds Accounting, which feeds Manufacturing costing - so scope creep compounds faster than in a single-module CRM rollout.

A multi-module implementation (Sales, Inventory, Accounting, CRM together) for a growing business runs ₹2,50,000 to ₹6,00,000. Manufacturing rollouts with custom development and multi-warehouse logistics run ₹6,00,000 to ₹15,00,000 and above.

4. The costs nobody quotes you

  • 18% GST on everything. A ₹3,00,000 implementation invoices at ₹3,54,000.
  • Year-two price rise. Odoo's attractive headline rate is usually a first-year promotion. Ask the partner for both the year-one and year-two per-user number before signing.
  • Server and hosting costs if self-hosting Community. A production-grade VPS with backups is a real, recurring line even though the licence itself is free.
  • GST and e-invoicing localisation testing. Odoo ships Indian tax modules, but they need validation against your actual GSTIN structure, not an assumption that they work untouched.
  • Opening stock and chart-of-accounts reconciliation. The single most underestimated migration task - budget time, not just a fee line.
  • Your own team's time. More departments touch Odoo than touch a CRM, so more internal hours go into UAT.
  • Rework from skipping discovery. Because Inventory, Accounting and Manufacturing are interdependent, a mis-scoped module causes knock-on rework in the others - more expensive to unwind than a single-module CRM mistake.

5. How to keep the number sensible

  1. Scope module-by-module, not "all of Odoo." Implement Sales and Inventory first, add Manufacturing or multi-company once the core is stable.
  2. Choose annual billing and lock in the first-year promotional rate in writing for year two as well, if the partner will agree to it.
  3. Insist on fixed-price scope per module, not one blended number for the whole project - it makes it obvious which module is driving the cost.
  4. Budget stock and accounts reconciliation as its own line item, not folded into "data migration," since it is usually the actual bottleneck.
  5. Do not cut training, especially for non-sales departments who are often newer to ERP software than a sales team is to a CRM.
  6. Phase the rollout by department. Inventory and Accounting first, Manufacturing once those are stable and trusted.

6. Questions worth asking any partner

Before signing anything, ask these. The answers separate consultants from resellers:

  • "Is this quote fixed-price per module, or time and material for the whole project?"
  • "What is the year-two per-user licence rate, in writing?"
  • "Who validates the GST/e-invoicing localisation against our actual tax scenarios?"
  • "Have you implemented Manufacturing (MRP), or only Sales/Inventory/Accounting?" - MRP is a genuinely different skill set.
  • "Show me a system you built for a business with our module mix."
  • "What happens to opening stock reconciliation if it runs over the estimated time?"

Frequently asked questions

How much does Odoo cost per user in India?

Odoo Community is free with no per-user fee, but you still pay for hosting and implementation. Odoo Enterprise (hosted as Standard or Custom) runs roughly ₹580 to ₹1,350 per user per month on annual billing, excluding 18% GST, depending on plan tier and whether it is the discounted first-year rate.

Is Odoo Community really free?

Yes for the licence itself: Community is open-source with no per-user fee. You still pay for a server (self-hosted or a VPS), and implementation work costs the same either way since the configuration and customisation effort does not change. Most growing businesses end up on Enterprise for official apps like full Accounting, Studio and native integrations that Community lacks.

What does Odoo implementation cost in India?

A lean single-module rollout (Inventory or Accounting only, under 10 users) typically runs ₹1,00,000 to ₹2,50,000. A multi-module implementation (Sales, Inventory, Accounting, CRM together) for a growing business runs ₹2,50,000 to ₹6,00,000. A manufacturing (MRP) rollout with multi-warehouse logistics and custom Python development runs ₹6,00,000 to ₹15,00,000 and above.

Is Odoo cheaper than Zoho One for a multi-app business?

Licence-for-licence Odoo's Enterprise per-user fee is often lower than stacking several separate Zoho apps, but the comparison only holds if you actually need Odoo's manufacturing and multi-warehouse depth. For a sales/CRM-led business without heavy inventory or MRP needs, Zoho typically reaches production faster and at a lower implementation cost, because its standard modules need less custom configuration.

Does Odoo implementation include GST and Indian compliance?

Odoo ships Indian localisation modules covering GST, e-invoicing and TDS, but they need to be configured and tested against your actual tax scenarios, not assumed to work out of the box. Budget for this as part of the Accounting module configuration line, particularly if you operate across multiple states with different GSTINs.

How long does an Odoo implementation take?

A single-module rollout for a small team takes three to six weeks. A multi-module implementation (Sales, Inventory, Accounting together) takes six to twelve weeks. Manufacturing deployments with custom Python development and multi-warehouse logistics run twelve to twenty-four weeks.
